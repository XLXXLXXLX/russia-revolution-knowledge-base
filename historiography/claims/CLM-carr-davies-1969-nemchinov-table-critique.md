## 核心论断命题（Thesis）
斯大林在1928年5月28日演说中援引中央统计局涅姆奇诺夫（V. S. Nemchinov）编制的谷物商品率统计表（显示全国谷物商品率从战前的 26% 降至1926–1927年的 13.3%，其中富农仅占商品粮的 20%，中农和贫农占 74%），试图以此论证“富农粮食罢工”与立即推进集体化的必要性；但 Carr 与 Davies 指出，商品率下降的真实结构性根源在于十月革命后农户（*dvor*）从战前 1600 万户裂变至 2560 万户的小农化及农村自身消费的改善，而涅姆奇诺夫表中“74% 商品粮掌握在中贫农手中”的数据恰恰戳穿了官方所谓“只打击富农即可解决粮食危机”的虚构——所谓“农村内部阶级斗争”的党内诊断在很大程度上是不真实的，真正的冲突发生在包括广大中农在内的全体余粮生产者与代表城市及工业化利益的国家机器之间。

## 论证链条与依据（Reasoning & Evidence）
1. **涅姆奇诺夫统计表的核心结构**：斯大林在1928年5月28日《在粮食战线上》演说中引用的涅姆奇诺夫表表明，战前地主提供 21.6% 的商品粮（商品率 47%）、富农提供 50%（商品率 34%）、中贫农提供 28.4%（商品率 14.7%）；而在1926–1927年度，国营农场和集体农庄仅提供 6% 的商品粮（商品率 47%），富农提供 20%（商品率 20%），**中农与贫农则提供了高达 74% 的商品粮**（尽管其自身商品率仅为 11.2%）`[p. 73 / PDF p. 88]`。
2. **商品率减半的制度与人口根源**：Carr 与 Davies 结合第4章《土地与农民》的统计指出，1917年土地革命消灭地主大庄园后，个体农户发生剧烈的分家裂变（*dvor* splitting），全苏农户总数从战前的约 1600 万户激增至1928年的 25,586,000 户（户均人口从 6.1 人降至 5.1 人），且每年仍以 2.5%–3% 的速度裂变 `[pp. 72, 117 / PDF pp. 87, 132]`。小农户优先改善自身口粮消费并用余粮饲养牲畜，加之农村人口较战前增长 15%，导致投向市场的谷物比例结构性萎缩 `[pp. 72, 244 / PDF pp. 87, 259]`。
3. **“只打击富农”神话的破产与城乡博弈实质**：既然 74% 的商品粮分散在中贫农手中，1928年初的“非常措施”就绝不可能像官方宣称的那样局限于占人口 3%–5% 的富农。米高扬于1928年2月10日在《真理报》上不得不承认：“绝大部分谷物余粮掌握在中农手中” `[p. 55 / PDF p. 70]`。Carr 与 Davies 在第11章总结指出，将危机解释为不同农民阶级之间的斗争是党内教条制造的假象，实质冲突在于谷物生产与分配究竟由拥有余粮的农民（包含大批中农）按自身利益控制，还是由代表城市和工厂利益的党政代理人控制 `[p. 246 / PDF p. 261]`。

## 原文直引（Verbatim Quote）
> "Noting that grain production had already reached pre-war levels, he faced the crucial issue of the marketed part of production. He quoted figures provided by Nemchinov of the Central Statistical Administration to show that, whereas before the war 26 per cent of the grain harvest was marketed, the marketed proportion of the grain harvest of 1926 had been only 13.3 per cent. Before the war 21.6 per cent of the marketed grain had come from landowners, 50 per cent from kulaks and 28.4 per cent from middle and poor peasants; in 1926-1927 6 per cent had come from Sovkhozy and kolkhozy, 20 per cent from the kulaks and 74 per cent from middle and poor peasants. On the other hand, Sovkhozy and kolkhozy marketed 47 per cent of their production, kulaks 20 per cent and middle and poor peasants only 11.2 per cent." `[p. 73]`
>
> "Official reports restricted the impact of the extraordinary measures to kulaks and 'well-to-do' peasants. But this fiction wore thin as time went on and the pace of the collections grew hotter. Mikoyan wrote with unwonted frankness in *Pravda* of February 10, 1928: 'The main mass of the grain surpluses was in the hands of middle peasants, who were often in no hurry to sell if they could not buy the equivalent quantity of manufactured goods which they wanted, or if they were not driven to do it by the need to pay their debts to the state or the cooperatives.'" `[p. 55]`
>
> "Interpreted in the sense of a struggle between different classes of peasant, this diagnosis, fostered by party doctrine, was largely unreal. The underlying issue at stake was whether the production and distribution of the grain should be controlled by, and in the interests of, the peasants who had surpluses of grain to bring to the market — and this category included a substantial proportion of middle peasants who could not be labelled as kulaks — or by agents of party and government who represented the interests of the towns and the factories; only thus far was it a class struggle in the conventional sense." `[p. 246]`
