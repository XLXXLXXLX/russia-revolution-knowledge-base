## 核心论断命题（Thesis）

俄国「革命」在时间与体验上应被理解为一个 **1905 至 1921 的关联危机连续体**：政治断裂（1905、1917 二月与十月、1918—1921 内战）并非孤立事件，而是同一长时段内人们在 **灾难、不确定、希望与英雄主义** 等复合情绪中反复阐释的「麻烦时代（times of trouble）」；报刊—记者「现在时史」与专业史家的回望叙事同样是在把破碎日常编排成意义，内战时期的 **兄弟阋墙、人身价值贬低与饥饿恐惧** 则把 1917 春之自由修辞重写为羞耻、流血与道德溃败的经验。

## 论证链条与依据（Reasoning & Evidence）

1. **编年即论点**：作者刻意拒绝仅取 1917 年 2—10 月等狭义时段，将 **1905–1921** 定义为 linked crises、剧变与可能性的时代；选择本身构成对革命终结点的史学立场 `[Intro. p. 19]`。
2. **当代词汇光谱**：当时人描述这些年月时用 smuta、catastrophe、uncertainty 与 heroism、hope、light 等 **不稳定、可彼此翻转** 的判断，个体情绪亦可骤变 `[Intro. pp. 19, 299–306]`。
3. **1905—1917 话语遗产**：1905 后记者将事件读作「新生活的黎明」与 **自由—人格—公民权** 的再生，同时忧虑大众暴力与「阴影」 `[Ch.2, pp. 49–62, 177–200]`——为 1917 再爆发提供情感与词汇储备。
4. **1917—1918 体验断崖**：独立左翼报纸 *News for Everyone* 专栏作家 Borchevsky 把 1917 总括为 **「year of catastrophes and disappointments」**：自由初期的「bright joy」后为政治冲突、Speech、十月，再为 **血流、莫斯科炮击、倚枪之五权政府**，及「no peace, no bread, no freedom」`[Ch.2 Part 3, pp. 147–160]`。
5. **暴力与生命贬值**：Gromov 等称革命 **「beginning to choke on the people's blood」**；内战中 civility 崩解被体验为 egoism、人身价值 falling、**「fratricidal」羞耻** `[pp. 171–181, 117–120]`。
6. **方法论重申**：报刊是已阐释的 trace，但体验史的价值正在听其 **如何将日常编为历史**，而非追求未媒介化的「真理」`[Intro. pp. 13–16]`。

## 原文直引（Verbatim Quote）

> "My book treats the years from 1905 to 1921 as an era of linked crises, upheavals, radical change, and possibility. My question, of course, is what this all meant to people at the time, how they saw the connections and ruptures." `[p. 19]`

> "And a great many people described something much less definite: a sense that they were living across these years in 'times of trouble,' 'catastrophe,' 'uncertainty,' 'confusion,' 'instability,' 'indeterminacy,' and 'groundlessness,' but also times of 'heroism,' 'hope,' 'light,' 'salvation,' and 'resurrection.' This was not a stable set of interpretations even for individuals: one judgment or mood could shift to another." `[p. 19]`

> "Blood pours in streams. Moscow burns and trembles under shelling. From this ocean of blood emerges a new government. With guns and bayonets in its hands. . . . But no peace, no bread, no freedom, no money, no work. . . . Only shame and fratricidal conflict. This is where we find ourselves now." `[p. 149]`

> "But the most terrible thing was that the revolution was beginning 'to choke on the people's blood,' to perish in the 'fratricidal whirlwind' of 'mutual destruction.'" `[p. 175]` (Steinberg summarizing Isaiah Gromov, March 1918)

> "How else could people free themselves from an old regime gripping desperately onto power with its 'old, bony fingers'?" `[p. 61]`
