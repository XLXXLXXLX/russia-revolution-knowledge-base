## 核心论断命题（Thesis）
凯文·麦克德莫特结合“格拉斯诺斯特”后莫斯科共产国际档案新成果，在博尔克瑙“斯大林事无巨细绝对控制论”与E.H.卡尔“缺席导演与自主空间论”之间作出了修正性综合。作者指出，1928—1929年反“右倾偏差”清洗（自德共维托夫事件、1928年12月19日执委主席团摊牌至1929年7月十次全会罢免布哈林及机关大清查）依托各国青年左派内生激进情绪，彻底奠定了斯大林对共产国际中央机关及支部领导层的人事与路线支配；但“社会法西斯主义”与“阶级对阶级”最初系吸纳欧洲左翼既有主张，斯大林因忙于一五计划与集体化亦非日常琐务微操者，而是拥有最终否决权与大政定调权的“遥远主宰”。在1930—1933年，共产国际虽日益服从避免激发西方干涉的苏联国家安全理性，但作为各国党组织网络仍保留了有限而真实的双轨张力。

## 论证链条与依据（Reasoning & Evidence）
1. **1928–1929 年反“右倾”清洗与机关斯大林化（Section I）**：“第三时期”最初由布哈林在1926年底七次扩大会提出，但1928年六大后转化为联共党内斗争的国际延伸。1928年8月斯大林电令皮亚特尼茨基将莫洛托夫塞入ECCI任政治局“看门狗”；9–10月借德共维托夫贪腐案强行复职台尔曼；12月19日斯、莫亲临ECCI主席团痛斥德共右派/调和派并辱骂安贝尔-德罗与塔斯卡（`f.558/f.495`）；1929年4月莫斯科美共委员会上斯大林以灭顶警告击败拥有正式多数的洛夫斯通派；5月首次引入GPU调查波共；7月十次全会正式罢免布哈林并加冕“社会法西斯主义”，继而1929年9月–1930年1月清洗ECCI机关`239`人（处分`31`人、开除联共党籍`7`人）`[pp. 410–418]`。
2. **基层激进响应与“窃用既定鹰派纲领”**：反右清算之所以奏效，不仅靠莫斯科铁拳，亦因各国青年团（如意共隆戈、捷共哥特瓦尔德、英共拉斯等）渴求摆脱NEP/统战僵局且1929年大萧条似印证崩溃论。斯大林并非亲自发明左转教条，而是适时窃用并钦定了莫洛托夫、洛佐夫斯基、库恩与台尔曼等鹰派先已形成的强硬主张`[pp. 412–413, 417–418, 422]`。
3. **档案证“总控绝对”与“日常缺席”并存（Section II）**：俄国学者菲尔索夫（Firsov）披露1933年底十三次全会皮亚特尼茨基向斯、莫、卡逐字送审提纲，证明无斯大林批准无任何重大政策或高层人事更替；然而卡尔“缺席导演”洞见仍有其理——斯大林重国内工业化，不亲理日常琐务，使ECCI内部存在强硬派与灵活派（马努伊尔斯基、库西宁等）分化`[pp. 419–422, 427]`。
4. **苏联国家安全与外交人民委员部（Narkomindel）的张力**：斯大林本人并不指望欧美起义成功，国际革命修辞一方面服务国内“世界工人拥护五年计划”的神话动员，另一方面受到国家安全严格约束：尽管1929年6月20日契切林致信斯大林痛斥“社会法西斯”之荒谬遭无视，但1930年起因惧怕英法反苏并保卫拉帕洛魏玛关系，莫斯科转而压制KPD“左倾冒险与个人恐怖”，反映出“莫斯科机关斯大林化”与“各国支部地面现实”之间微弱却真实的双重性`[pp. 423–426]`。

## 原文核验引文（Verbatim Quotes）
> "Borkenau states categorically that during the third period, 'Stalin kept an iron control over all details of Comintern work, and both the policy and the lists of parliamentary candidates of the communist parties were rigidly controlled by Moscow'. Carr begs to differ, arguing that 'Stalin, heavily engaged elsewhere, was not tempted to concern himself with the petty disputes of an institution he had always despised.' Thus, 'it would be misleading to depict Comintern and its component parties in the early nineteen-thirties as a monolithic structure responding blindly to the dictates of a single supreme authority.' It would be hard to imagine a more unequivocal clash of opinion. But which interpretation is closer to reality?" `[p. 419 / PDF p. 11]`

> "Where he [Carr] is perhaps on more solid ground is in his depiction of Stalin as 'an absentee director who occasionally turns up unexpectedly and demands that props be removed or the odd actor ... replaced, and then disappears, leaving others to cope with the mess.' Stalin, weighed down with the massive burdens of the First Five-Year Plan and the ongoing agricultural crisis, had neither the time nor the inclination to indulge in day-to-day supervision of the ECCI. Borkenau's claim that Stalin controlled 'all details' of the Comintern's work is thus an exaggeration." `[p. 422 / PDF p. 14]`

> "Is it possible that in 1929, Stalin, rather than initiating the left turn, actually appropriated and sanctioned the uncompromising positions of others, 'hawks' such as Molotov, Lozovsky, Kun and Thälmann? This appears to be the case with the 'social fascist' slogan, which almost definitely did not originate with Stalin, but which was canonized by him in the course of that year." `[p. 422 / PDF p. 14]`

> "But it should be re-emphasized that in the early 1930s the tactics of the Comintern were not exclusively defined by the needs of Soviet diplomacy. National and local conditions impinged on the elaboration of policies on the ground. A certain duality existed, always muted, but nonetheless real. In this sense the Comintern cannot be called a mere instrument of the Soviet state, if by the word 'Comintern' we mean an international organization of Communist parties as distinct from the Stalinized apparatus in Moscow." `[p. 426 / PDF p. 18]`

> "The received wisdom that Stalin decided everything may need revising. He was a more distant ruler than this interpretation would allow. It is feasible, moreover, that the details of the third period line were elaborated by others, both within the Comintern hierarchy and the national parties. But, given the recent research of Russian specialists, it would be a brave historian who denied that Stalin maintained a decisive influence over the determination of the general strategies, and, crucially, pronounced them irrevocable and universal. When he decided to speak, his word was gospel. It is clear that no major policy innovation was possible without his direct intervention or sanction, and nor was any change in the composition of Communist party leaderships. We do not need to regard the Stalin of 1929-33 as an omnipotent tyrant to appreciate the extent of his mastery over the Comintern." `[p. 427 / PDF p. 19]`
