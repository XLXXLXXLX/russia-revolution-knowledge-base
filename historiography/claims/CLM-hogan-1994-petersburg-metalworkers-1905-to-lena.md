## 核心论断命题（Thesis）
海瑟·霍根（Heather Hogan）在《圣彼得堡金属工业的阶级形成：从“自由的日子”到连纳金矿惨案》中论证：**1912–1914 年彼得格勒金属工人的爆发性激进抗争，并非突然降临或纯由党派煽动所致，而是 1905 年十月宣言之后（1905–1912）三个相互叠加的冲突场域长期塑造的结果**：（1）生产现场中从“自由的日子”管理权威崩塌到斯托雷平反动期雇主反扑、引入泰勒制与去人格化“体制”的拉锯；（2）市政层面上 1906 年失业者委员会围绕公共工程同市杜马对抗，促使工人将厂主与“纳税资格社会（*census society*）”视为一体化的敌对“他们”；（3）国家层面上 `1905-12-11 [O.S.] / 1905-12-24 [N.S.]` 选举法依托工厂设立的“工人选民团（*workers' curia*）”，在制度上将“工人”与更宽泛的“公民”身份剥离，从而使社会民主党的阶级分析框架在连纳惨案前夕获得压倒性的说服力。

## 论证链条与依据（Reasoning & Evidence）
1. **场域一（前段）：1905 年“自由的日子”管理危机与 1906 年夏防御性罢工**（`orig. pp. 86–95` / `PDF pp. 103–112`）：
   - 自 `1905-10-17 [O.S.] / 1905-10-30 [N.S.]` 至 `1905-12-16 [O.S.] / 1905-12-29 [N.S.]` 莫斯科普列斯尼亚区遭炮击为止的六周“自由的日子”里，金属工人在食堂与厂院举行无休止集会、自造武器并单方面推行 8 小时工作制，直接击垮底层工长权威；11 月至 12 月国家（海军部波罗的海厂、奥布霍夫厂、伊若拉厂）与 72 家私营金属厂联合关厂封厂与政治甄别（*filtrovka*），重创 8 小时运动（`pp. 86–88`）。
   - `1906-06–08 [O.S.] / 1906-06–09 [N.S.]` 爱立信（Erikson）、阿特拉斯（Atlas）等五家中型机械厂爆发围绕解雇、按小时计酬替代日薪及保护工间休息的抗争，预示管理层已开始通过改组劳动过程来削弱车间自治（`pp. 88–95`）。
2. **场域二：1906 年失业者委员会对抗市杜马与“他们 vs. 我们”的城市极化**（`orig. pp. 95–103` / `PDF pp. 112–120`）：
   - `1906-03-22 [O.S.] / 1906-04-04 [N.S.]` 成立的圣彼得堡失业者委员会（核心多为 30 岁以下、识字、高薪的熟练青年金属工，已无农村退路）于 `1906-04-12 [O.S.] / 1906-04-25 [N.S.]` 迫使市杜马拨款 50 万卢布并承诺兴办公共工程；但 `1906-07-09 [O.S.] / 1906-07-22 [N.S.]` 第一届国家杜马遭解散后，城市精英政治恐慌消退，市杜马与实业家（如普梯洛夫厂长别洛诺日金）联手撤回救济并扼杀公共工程（`pp. 96–102`）。
   - “要工作不要施舍”的抗争经历使工人将市杜马“纳税人绅士”与私营厂主完全等同，提前预演了 1917 年“他们（资产阶级/纳税资格社会）vs. 我们（城市底层 *nizy*/工人阶级）”的二元结构（`pp. 98–99, 102–103`）。
3. **场域三：1905 年 12 月选举法“工人选民团”与公民身份的缺席**（`orig. pp. 103–104` / `PDF pp. 120–121`）：
   - `1905-12-11 [O.S.] / 1905-12-24 [N.S.]` 颁布的国家杜马选举法不以个人公民资格、而以连续在雇佣至少 50 名男工的工厂工作满 6 个月作为登记门槛，在工厂内部逐级隔离投票，把全国政治过程与车间阶级归属严丝合缝地焊在一起，令立宪民主党（Kadets）等自由派彻底丧失工人票源，积累起对整个现存秩序的愤懑。
4. **场域一（后段）：斯托雷平反动期泰勒制、“非人格化”权威与工人报刊的阶级伦理**（`orig. pp. 105–112` / `PDF pp. 122–129`）：
   - 1907–1912 年雇主趁经济停滞取消“工厂宪法”，广泛引入秒表测时、自动打卡钟（“红线”迟到惩罚）、独立定额局与成文雇佣规章，剥夺饮茶吸烟与“自由间隙”，并用女工、童工、杂工顶替熟练 *slesari*；然而，管理层试图以“客观系统（system）”替代工长主观裁量来平息矛盾，在工人眼中却适得其反地掩盖了降薪与裁员的问责链条（`pp. 105–109`）。
   - 在自由派与激进知识分子撤退后的孤立状态中，金属工会刊物（*Kuznets*、*Edinstvo*、*Metallist*）背面登载的大量厂区通讯员来信谴责加班讨好、抢活压价、酗酒与向工长送礼/供奉圣像灯油钱（*lampadnye kassy*），反向锻造出强调尊严、团结与自主的“无产阶级伦理”。由此，当 `1912-04-04 [O.S.] / 1912-04-17 [N.S.]` 西伯利亚连纳金矿屠杀消息于 4 月下旬传入彼得格勒时，蓄积已久的阶级愤怒瞬间引爆。

## 原文直引（Verbatim Quote）
> "In the years 1912–14, labor protest exploded in the metalworking industry of St. Petersburg. I argue that this extraordinary activism was shaped by a series of events in the aftermath of the October Manifesto of 1905, which created a context in which metalworkers increasingly chose to define themselves in class terms. In this process of class formation three sites of political conflict were especially important: at the point of production, as reflected in the collapse of managerial authority over the course of 1905, but most especially during the 'days of freedom'; at the municipal level, as highlighted by the struggle between the Council of the Unemployed and the St. Petersburg City Duma over a program of public works, but which raised a series of community issues more generally; and in the workers' curias, as manifested in the elections for the State Duma in 1906." `[orig. p. 85 | PDF p. 102 | ch08_3_class_formation_in_the_st_petersburg_m.md#L12-L23]`

> "Over the course of 1906, class and community were thus linked in new ways; and as new conflicts were joined, all involved were unavoidably forced to view power, privilege, class, and estate from a different perspective. In its reconceptualization of the 'enemy' the Petersburg unemployed movement foreshadowed one of the structuring realities of 1917—a conflict in which 'they' (census society, but also and equally bourgeois society) were pitted against 'us' (the urban nizy [lower strata], but also and equally the working class)." `[orig. p. 98 | PDF p. 115 | ch08_3_class_formation_in_the_st_petersburg_m.md#L628-L636]`

> "Politics and a particular social identity were thus neatly fused in a multitiered electoral system that began in the shop and at each subsequent stage emphasized the distinctiveness of the worker as well as minimized the opportunity for discussion and association across class lines. Meeting in segregated groups provided little chance for one group to encounter the discourse of another. Thus the worker remained a worker, not a citizen with other sources of identity beyond or in distinction to his identity at the workplace." `[orig. p. 104 | PDF p. 121 | ch08_3_class_formation_in_the_st_petersburg_m.md#L917-L924]`

> "From labor's point of view, this impersonality obscured accountability: it was no longer clear who had cut their rates, hired the adolescents to do the work of slesari, or fired the senior workers. And so when workers struck out at supervisory personnel or at the pace of work they compelled, workers were striking out more clearly than in the past at 'the system'—at the reconceived managerial structures in which individual foremen had little remaining responsibility (or authority) on the shop floor." `[orig. p. 108 | PDF p. 125 | ch08_3_class_formation_in_the_st_petersburg_m.md#L1120-L1126]`

> "The historically specific experiences of metalworkers on the shop floor, in the city, and in the national electoral process over the 1906–12 period made the social democrats' class-based analysis of these experiences particularly compelling. In isolation and in difficult material conditions, St. Petersburg workers repressed their rage and punished themselves with destructive bouts of drinking and exhausting hours of labor; but they also began to conceive their world in stark opposition to the rest of Petersburg society. The explosive protests surrounding the Lena Goldfields massacre would suggest the degree to which the experiences of this difficult period between late 1905 and early 1912 had shaped metalworker thinking." `[orig. pp. 111–112 | PDF pp. 128–129 | ch08_3_class_formation_in_the_st_petersburg_m.md#L1288-L1310]`
