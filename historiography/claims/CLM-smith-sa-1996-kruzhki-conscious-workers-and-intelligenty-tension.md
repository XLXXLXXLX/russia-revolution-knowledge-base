## 核心论断命题（Thesis）

在 1895–1917 年的圣彼得堡，通过主日学校、夜校、马克思主义小组（*kruzhki*）与工人俱乐部成长起来的“觉悟工人（*soznatel'nye rabochie* / worker-intelligentsia）”——主体为 16–20 岁进城、未婚、高识字率的青年熟练金属工与印刷工——既拒斥“灰色工人群体（grey worker mass）”的粗鄙麻木，又渴望进入知识分子所代表的经典文学与科学世界（*kul'turnost'*），从而处于两个文化地带的**阈限状态（liminal state）**。这种以个体人格尊严（*lichnost'*）为核心的自我觉醒，使他们与党内外知识分子之间产生了持久而充满矛盾的张力：冲突焦点从 1897–1907 年反抗知识分子对领导职位的“沙皇式”垄断，演变为 1907 年后要求亲自掌控工人教育内容、痛恨流亡知识分子“保姆式”的宗派内斗，以及因反动年代（1907–1912）知识分子大举脱党而产生的被“导师—父亲”抛弃的**“精神孤儿化（psychic orphaning）”**创伤。然而，这种从盲目理想化到幻灭愤懑的剧烈冲突，客观上却成为一种促成党内工—知平等关系的健康力量。

## 论证链条与依据（Reasoning & Evidence）

1. **“觉悟工人”的社会学画像与“阈限状态”**：自 1870 年代民粹派组织工人小组（*kruzhki*）及 1880 年代中期社会民主党兴起以来，“觉悟工人”主要源自年轻、单身、具有一定读写能力的农村进城青年，集中在机械制造（金属）、印刷、木工、裁缝等高技能行业（`[p. 9]`）。他们以对个人尊严（*lichnost'*）与自尊的执着来对抗大多数工人的酗酒、赌博与屈辱生活，但也因此遭到“灰色工人群体”的嫌恶，同时在面对知识分子时又深感文化自卑与生存焦虑，身处不舒适的“跨文化边界”（`[pp. 9–11]`）。
2. **1905 年后从被动受教到争夺文化与教育自主权**：1905 年革命（`1905-01-09 [O.S.] / 1905-01-22 [N.S.]` 流血星期日至 `1905-10-17 [O.S.] / 1905-10-30 [N.S.]` 十月宣言）后，特别是在 1907–1914 年圣彼得堡兴起的 20 多个工人俱乐部（如“知识就是光明”俱乐部，5,000–6,000 名会员）中，工人不再甘于知识分子的监护（tutelary relationship），要求自己掌控教育的形式与内容，并在 `1908年1月 [O.S.]` 全俄人民大学活动家第一次代表大会上要求知识分子承认工人必须自己发展“阶级知识”（`[pp. 6–7]`）。
3. **党内劳动分工与日常社交屈辱**：布尔什维克与社会民主党内不同技能造成的分工将知识分子与“言辞（the word）”绑定、将工人与“行动（the deed）”绑定（`[p. 18]`）。Frolov 在教授安全屋里因不会用西餐刀叉与餐巾而感到自己像个“食客（hanger-on）”，Kanatchikov 反感自由派知识分子把革命工人带回家“展览”（`[pp. 18–19]`）。
4. **冲突焦点的四重历史演进**：
   - **领导权垄断与“沙皇式”指派（1897–1907）**：从 1897 年 10 月《工人思想报》挑战忏悔知识分子领导权，到 1905 年三大上 Krasikov 痛批党内委员会易纳女学生（*kursistka*）而拒纳工人的“恐工症（*rabocheboiazn'*）”，再到 `1905-05-01 [O.S.] / 1905-05-14 [N.S.]` 五一游行后工人抗议外派知识分子在区委“当沙皇”（`[pp. 19–21]`）。
   - **反抗流亡知识分子的宗派分裂（1907–1917）**：1907 年后工人进入地方领导层，矛盾转向痛恨知识分子热衷意识形态纯洁性与宗派内斗（Glebov-Putilovskii 拒斥“知识分子保姆”，1912 年金属工人工会积极分子拒斥“来自国外的布道”）；对工人而言，派系倾轧违背了社会主义赖以立足的团结与平等准则（`[pp. 21–22]`）。
   - **通识自主教育 vs 实用党务训练**：列宁强调将工人训练成与知识分子在“党务活动”上平起平坐的职业鼓动员与组织者，而波格丹诺夫与卢那察尔斯基在卡普里与博洛尼亚党校则致力于培养能独立思考的“工人知识分子”，反映出觉悟工人渴望摆脱对党内知识分子智识依赖的诉求（`[pp. 22–23]`）。
   - **不对称的地下生存代价与“精神孤儿化”**：脱产工人全赖每月 35 卢布“党津贴”，无知识分子的家庭接济、稿费外快、克里米亚暑期休假与被捕优待，且面临与不识字妻子的家庭撕裂（`[pp. 23–25]`）。1907–1912 年反动年代知识分子大举逃离革命阵营，被工人视为“导师—父亲的遗弃（psychic orphaning）”，激起 Vilonov 等工人哲学家痛斥知识分子为“掠夺者（*marodery*）”的强烈反智情绪（`[p. 27]`）。

> "Yet these clubs were subtly different from the evening classes at the turn of the century, since workers now aspired to control the form and content of their education. [...] Militants, mostly Social Democrats, resented the tutelary relationship to the latter and sought to define the object of educational and cultural work for themselves. [...] Workers were no longer ready to accept that the intelligentsia knew best or to feel grateful for its intellectual and moral leadership." `[pp. 6–7]`

> "In the main, they were like those who later joined the workers' clubs: young men (there were 'conscious' working women, but they were few), born in the countryside, who had migrated to the city at the age of sixteen to twenty, still unmarried. Possessed of a degree of literacy, they gained skilled, relatively well-paid jobs in factory or artisanal trades such as engineering, printing, carpentry or tailoring. In terms of values, they believed passionately in individuality (*lichnost'*) and self-respect. [...] In their determination to repudiate the world of the 'grey' worker, in their anxiety to enter the world of the intelligentsia, 'conscious' workers were in a liminal state, on the border of two cultural zones." `[pp. 9–11]`
> —— 源：`ch00_pages_1-25.md#L478-L487, L560-L595`

> "In practice, worker resentment at intelligentsia predominance in the party remained a persistent feature of Social Democracy down to 1917. This tension had its roots in the different social and cultural backgrounds of the two groups, and in the fact that the different 'skills' of each tended to create a division of labour within the party which aligned *intelligenty* with the word, and workers with the deed. Nevertheless the issues which sparked worker antagonism changed over time." `[p. 18]`
> —— 源：`ch00_pages_1-25.md#L1017-L1024`

> "Tension between *intelligenty* and workers within the RSDLP did not abate with the 1905 Revolution. After the failure of the May Day demonstration, a worker told an SD meeting: 'As soon as we form a district organization an intellectual is immediately sent in from somewhere to act as tsar. Undoubtedly, in some districts we have good tsars, but there are some who won't let us open our mouths. We need neither good nor bad tsars, we just want to run our own party.'" `[pp. 20–21]`
> —— 源：`ch00_pages_1-25.md#L1183-L1203`

> "The advance of workers into leadership after 1907 removed this particular bone of contention. Yet conflict between *intelligenty* and workers persisted. Its focus, however, now shifted to the issue of the supposed propensity of the intelligentsia to sectarian wrangling. [...] N.N. Glebov-Putilovskii averred that 'we do not need intellectual nannies to befuddle the workers with sectarian nonsense'; in 1912 an activist in the St Petersburg union of metalworkers declared: 'Lenin and Plekhanov are good so long as they confine themselves to the realm of political economy and philosophy', but the workers organizations 'do not need sermons from abroad'. [...] Whereas for intellectuals, ideological conflict was the guarantee of correct, scientific politics, for workers such conflict breached the code of solidarity and equality on which socialism was based." `[pp. 21–22]`
> —— 源：`ch00_pages_1-25.md#L1220-L1239, L1270-L1272`

> "The exodus of *intelligenty* from the revolutionary camp during the Years of Reaction left a permanent scar on the psyche of these worker Bolsheviks. In their eyes, the desertion was more than a *trahison des clercs*: it signified abandonment by their mentor-fathers, a kind of psychic orphaning. This helps to explain the fierceness of much anti-intelligentsia sentiment in this period, the tirades against their irresponsibility and untrustworthiness, such as those of another worker-philosopher, N.E. Vilonov, who in September 1909 denounced intellectuals for being 'pillagers' (*marodery*) of the party." `[p. 27]`
> —— 源：`ch01_pages_26-50.md#L85-L95`

> "Looked at from a psychological point of view, we can see that the relationship between workers and intellectuals was shot through with ambivalence, suffused with elements of fantasy and projection. It could thus switch rapidly from credulous idealization to bitter disillusionment. Yet as we shall suggest in respect of relations between workers and intellectuals in the CCP, the fact that this relationship was fraught with conflict may have been a healthy development which helped to create a more equal relationship between the two groups." `[p. 27]`

## 语境说明与局限提示

- **回忆录史料的解释张力**：Smith 本人在注释中坦承，其倚重的工人回忆录（Kanatchikov、Frolov、Shapovalov、Fisher、Buiko、Ivanov 等）在评估工—知冲突严重程度上存在差异（如孟什维克 Lidia Dan 坚决否认工人普遍敌视知识分子，Zelnik 亦指出 Kanatchikov 与 Fisher 的态度差异），且 1917 年布尔什维克党内的反智张力可能比整个俄国社会层面的反资产阶级/反知识分子狂潮更为温和（`[p. 18, n.89]`）。
- **性别与族群维度的交叉**：女性党员（约占 15%）绝大多数为知识女性（1902–1917 年 PK 30 名女性中 26 人为 *intelligentki*），在《女工（*Rabotnitsa*）》周围与女工合作较融洽；相反，领导层中非俄罗斯族（尤其犹太知识分子）比例偏高（1917 年 PK 82 人中含 22 名犹太人），在不必然涉及反犹主义的前提下加深了与俄罗斯族工厂工人之间的社会距离感（`[pp. 15–17]`）。
